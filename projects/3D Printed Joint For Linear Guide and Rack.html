PROJECT CASE STUDY

3D Printed Joint For Linear Guide and Rack

SolidWorks, M1 Rack, Linear Guide

A 1cm part had to connect to another 1cm part. There was no room for a normal joint. So I redesigned the entire approach It is infact a small component, but a critical one. If this joint failed, neither system would have passed testing.

INTRODUCTION

Project Overview

While building two of my engineering projects, I ran into a deceptively simple problem. I needed to connect a rack to a linear guide, but the rack itself was only 1 centimeter in dimension, and the available mounting space on the linear guide was also exactly 1 centimeter. A standard single-body joint simply couldn't fit which later led me create a joint and 3D print the boides to solve the problem.

3D Printed Joint For Linear Guide and Rack.

3D Printed Linear Guide And Rack Joint

The custom 3D-printed joint features a specialized two-piece split construction, departing from traditional single-body designs to overcome severe spatial limitations. Standard solid-joint configurations are physically incompatible with the hardware due to the structural envelope of the 1 cm Module 1 (M1) rack, which leaves only 1 cm of clearance once the mounting screws are in place. By split-coupling the joint into two interlocking components, the design bypasses these clearance constraints, allowing secure fastening within a tight mounting footprint. This precise structural arrangement rigidly anchors the rack to the linear guide, ensuring low-friction linear translation and preventing mechanical deflection for highly efficient rack-and-pinion engagement.

Horizontal Operation for the joint

A dedicated rack-and-pinion mechanism integrated with a gearbox was implemented to achieve precise rotational movements in vertical operation. The rack-and-pinion assemblies are driven directly through the gear transmission, converting vertical linear translation into controlled angular motion. This mechanical arrangement reliably actuates the left and right acrylic flaps along the vertical axis, ensuring smooth, stable, and repeatable angular movement under load.

Vertical Operation for the joint

The rotation of the flaps is essential for the folding process, and a dedicated rack-and-pinion gear system was implemented to achieve these rotational movements. The rack-and-pinion assemblies are mounted vertically on the lower platform and linked to the double-acting pneumatic cylinders so that the vertical linear motion of the cylinder shafts is converted into precise rotational motion. This vertical arrangement drives the left and right acrylic flaps, ensuring a controlled, stable, and repeatable angular translation necessary for the initial folding stages.

RESULTS

Research Outcome

Ultimately, the design performed reliably in both vertical and horizontal orientations and was successfully integrated into two separate projects: the Kinetic Energy Harvesting System and the Pneumatic Shirt Folding System. Both initiatives operated under stringent deadlines and depended on this joint to meet their performance requirements.
