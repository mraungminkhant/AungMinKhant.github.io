PROJECT CASE STUDY

GPS Linear Interpolation

Neo-6M GPS Module, ESP32, Google Sheet, MATLAB, Google My Map

This project involves acquiring GPS coordinates (latitude and longitude) from a Neo-6M GPS module and processing the position data in MATLAB to perform linear interpolation for route analysis.

INTRODUCTION

Project Overview

This project involves acquiring GPS coordinates (latitude and longitude) from a Neo-6M GPS module and processing the position data in MATLAB to perform linear interpolation for route analysis. The system collects time-stamped location fixes, filters noisy satellite readings, and calculates intermediate points along a track. MATLAB routines visualize the trajectory, compare actual and estimated paths, and validate the interpolation algorithm. Results show improved route estimation when GPS points are sparse, supporting navigation studies and analysis in a compact workflow.

Unprocessed GPS coordinates

The unprocessed GPS coordinates were acquired continuously across the university campus using a Neo-6M GPS module. These raw latitude and longitude readings captured positional data at successive intervals while the receiver traversed academic buildings, pathways, and open grounds. Each coordinate pair retained its original accuracy and noise characteristics, representing real-world signal reception without smoothing or filtering. This dataset enables analysis of spatial variation, satellite geometry, and environmental impact on GPS performance across diverse campus locations. ESP32 microcontroller was utilized to send the obtained GPS coordinates to Google Sheet to record them for later analysis with MATLAB.

Tall Structures Affecting Accuracy

Near tall buildings, signals bounce off surfaces before reaching the receiver. Because they arrive late, they cause the module to calculate positions that are physically wrong by over 150 meters. This phenomenon, known as multipath error, is one of the most significant sources of GPS inaccuracy in urban environments. Dense urban canyons with skyscrapers and reflective surfaces amplify this problem significantly. Additionally, atmospheric interference and ionospheric delays can further degrade positional accuracy, making precise GPS measurements extremely challenging in such areas.

Coordinates After Linear Interpolation

The obtained GPS coordinates were processed in MATLAB, where interpolation was used to correct the raw positional data and produce a more accurate track. The coordinates were refined by applying linear interpolation to the recorded latitude and longitude samples, compensating for missing points and noisy measurements. Because the Earth is not flat, interpolation was performed on a spherical surface (along great‑circle/geodesic paths) rather than as simple Cartesian straight lines, ensuring more accurate intermediate positions. The processing of MATLAB smoothed the trajectory and improved the consistency of the reconstructed path. This corrected coordinate set provided a reliable basis for subsequent mapping and analysis of the GPS data.

RESULTS

Research Outcome

After correction, the coordinates are realigned and the path is restored. It's a software-level correction that requires no hardware changes. For structured navigation environments such as campus paths, warehouse aisles, or fixed outdoor routes, it's a practical and implementable solution. Understanding where a sensor fails is just as important as understanding how it works.
